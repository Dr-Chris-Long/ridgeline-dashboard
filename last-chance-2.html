Analysis

What we did, and what it proves

RIDGELINE ran a multi-model adversarial compliance review against the Last Chance Forest Management Project Environmental Assessment — before the [REDACTED] complaint was filed — and independently reconstructed the litigation architecture that plaintiffs subsequently brought.

What we did. Two runs were executed against the same EA. The v5 baseline (run 2822839a, 2026-04-13) evaluated the full 390-cluster regulatory obligation catalog across every resource category. The v6 proof-of-concept (run f63a46b0, 2026-05-14) re-ran the pipeline on hardened, provenance-pinned code (commit 0f0166c) against the 40 Northern Spotted Owl obligation clusters. Each cluster is argued by an adversarial triad — a plaintiff model constructs the strongest litigation attack, a defense model rebuts from the administrative record, and an arbiter resolves — then findings are severity-graded, classified, and assembled into cascade chains that model how individual weaknesses compound into vacatur risk.

What it proves. Three things, each independently verifiable from the substrate:

1. The methodology is uncontaminated. The complaint never entered the model context, confirmed three independent ways (below). The system did not read the lawsuit and echo it back — it derived the same attack surface from the EA and the regulatory catalog alone.

2. The methodology is predictive. The v5 cascade taxonomy — authored before the complaint — maps to all six attack vectors [REDACTED] ultimately filed, including the multi-statute structure of the complaint itself.

3. The methodology is reproducible. v6 reproduced the v5 NSO findings on hardened code with full provenance pinning, holding 23 of 40 clusters stable and explaining every divergence by mechanism.

Defensible audit statement — substrate-locked (decision 216)
RIDGELINE v5, run blind against the Last Chance EA without complaint text in the model context (verifiable from code at commit 0f0166c and from a zero-citation scan of all findings), identified the multi-vector litigation architecture that [REDACTED] subsequently filed. Divergence Mechanism

The two RED→GREEN flips

Both flips share one mechanism. The plaintiff argument is identical across v5 and v6; only the defense posture changed. In v6 the defense invoked “the analysis exists in tiered BA / BiOp / Appendix C documents not shown in the EA excerpt,” the classifier labeled the finding DOCUMENTATION_GAP, and the recalibration map translated that to GREEN.

OC-NSO-008 · Recently Occupied Site Protection (5yr)

v5: defense CONCUR_RED → RED
v6: defense REBUT_STRONG → GREEN (DOCUMENTATION_GAP)

OC-NSO-026 · NSO Seasonal Disturbance Timing

v5: defense FIX_BEFORE_DECISION_PI → RED
v6: defense REBUT_STRONG → GREEN (DOCUMENTATION_GAP)

Neither flipped cluster appears as a named claim in the filed complaint, so the GREEN calls are defensible against this complaint. But the GREEN reasoning depends on the BA/BiOp being adequate — precisely what [REDACTED] Claim 2 attacks. Decision 215 (pending) would default DOCUMENTATION_GAP to YELLOW rather than GREEN, holding these as flag-pending-AR-verification and restoring v5 behavioral alignment.
